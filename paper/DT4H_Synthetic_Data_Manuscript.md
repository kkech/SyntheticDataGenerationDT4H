# A privacy-validated synthetic version of the DataTools4Heart heart-failure cohort, with a calibrated evaluation and gated-release pipeline

## Abstract

Patient-level cardiology data are difficult to share, which slows method development and reproducibility. We release synthetic versions of the DataTools4Heart (DT4H) Use Case 1 heart-failure cohort (4,694 patients, 249 variables spanning demographics, conditions, medications, laboratory values, vital signs, and five-year outcomes), generated by six model families with and without differential privacy (DP; ε ∈ {1, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20}) across replicate seeds — 27 released datasets in total. Uniquely, every generator was trained on a 75% split, with the remaining 1,174 patients held out to calibrate all evaluations: fidelity is read against the sampling-noise floor between two real samples, utility is tested on unseen patients, and privacy is assessed with a four-attack suite (membership inference, attribute inference, singling-out, linkability) whose null is the holdout itself. All attacks performed at chance level for every dataset (mean membership-inference AUC = 0.500), with zero verbatim records and zero records closer to the training data than unseen real patients. Fidelity, however, dissociates by capability: marginal-based DP synthesis reproduces categorical distributions and Kaplan–Meier survival curves indistinguishably from real unseen patients but fabricates cross-column associations and violates row-level clinical logic, while neural non-DP synthesis shows the opposite profile. We quantify this dissociation with a novel row-coherence audit (363 rules mined from the real data), and release the complete pipeline, whereby every number, figure, and document regenerates from one command, together with an automated release gate that all published files passed. The missingness of clinical data — which carries meaning — is preserved end-to-end by a sentinel encoding that is decoded back to null in the released files.

## Background & Summary

Heart failure is a leading cause of hospitalization and mortality, and data-driven research on heart-failure cohorts is constrained by legitimate privacy protections on patient-level electronic health record (EHR) data. Synthetic data — artificial records drawn from a generative model of the real cohort — has been proposed as a release mechanism that preserves statistical utility without exposing individuals (Chen et al., 2021; Gonzales et al., 2023). Yet published synthetic-data releases often share three weaknesses: fidelity is reported without a reference for how much two *real* samples differ, privacy is asserted from the generation mechanism rather than tested by attack, and evaluation stops at column distributions without asking whether individual synthetic rows are clinically coherent patients.

This Data Descriptor releases synthetic versions of the DataTools4Heart (DT4H) Use Case 1 (UC1) heart-failure cohort and addresses all three weaknesses by design:

1. **A held-out real sample calibrates everything.** Generators were trained on 3,520 patients; 1,174 patients were held out before any generation. Every fidelity metric is read against the *sampling-noise floor* — the same metric computed between the training and holdout samples — every utility result is tested on the holdout, and every privacy attack uses the holdout as its population of real non-members.
2. **Privacy is attacked, not asserted.** Beyond distance-to-closest-record analysis, we run the four standard adversarial evaluations — membership inference, attribute inference, singling-out, and linkability (Giomi et al., 2023) — on all 27 released datasets.
3. **Evaluation extends to row-level clinical coherence.** We mine 363 logical rules from the real data (e.g., cause-specific death implies all-cause death; CKD stage must match the patient's own eGFR value) and report each dataset's violation rate against the real holdout's own rate — real clinical data violates its own logic occasionally, and that is the fair bar.

The release covers six generative model families — Gaussian copula, TVAE, and CTGAN (Patki et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2019) without formal privacy guarantees, and MST (McKenna et al., 2021), AIM (McKenna et al., 2022), and DP-CTGAN (Rosenblatt et al., 2020; SmartNoise) with (ε, δ)-differential privacy (Dwork & Roth, 2014) — across a privacy-budget sweep ε ∈ {1, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20} and replicate seeds, for 27 released datasets from 31 planned runs (AIM exceeded its compute budget above ε = 5; see Technical Validation).

Beyond the data themselves, two findings characterize the current state of tabular synthesis on real clinical data. First, capabilities dissociate: the marginal-based DP method (MST) reproduces categorical distributions *below* the sampling-noise floor and produces five-year Kaplan–Meier curves statistically indistinguishable from unseen real patients, yet fabricates thousands of cross-column associations and fails a row-coherence release gate; the neural non-DP method (TVAE) shows the near-opposite profile. No evaluated model delivers marginal fidelity, joint structure, and row coherence simultaneously — under DP or otherwise. Second, privacy and fidelity did not trade off in the attack results: the best-fitting models were exactly as unattackable as the worst, empirically supporting the premise that population-level learning does not require individual-level memorization.

All results in this paper regenerate from a single command in the released pipeline, which also produced every figure, the per-column codebook, and this datasheet's technical content.

## Methods

### Source data

The source cohort comprises 4,694 adult heart-failure patients extracted from the EHR of a DT4H clinical partner site under the project's federated protocol, using the standardized onFHIR/Feast feature-extraction toolchain and the UC1 feature-set definition (528 declared variables). Data were delivered into the project's secure research environment in August 2026 (as dated in the recorded provenance); all processing, including generator training, occurred inside that environment, and only aggregate statistics, synthetic records, and reports leave it. Each row is one patient (source identifiers were verified 100% unique before removal).

The extracted table spans demographics; encounter characteristics; 30 symptom indicators; 108 medication and 78 condition indicators; laboratory values, vital signs, echocardiography and electrocardiography measurements (first/last values within the observation window); NYHA class; and five-year follow-up outcomes: cause-specific death and rehospitalization flags in windows from 7 days to 5 years, plus days-to-event columns.

### Preprocessing: no imputation, missingness as information

Clinical missingness carries meaning: a null days-to-death means the patient did not die within follow-up; a null laboratory value means the test was not ordered, itself a clinical signal. The pipeline therefore never imputes. Instead:

- **Numeric nulls** are encoded as a per-column *sentinel* placed below the observed range (offset = max(25% of the range, 1.0)), so generators model missingness jointly with values; after generation, any value below the column's observed minimum decodes back to null. The full encoding map (per-column sentinel, observed minimum, and a structural/"not measured" label) is released as public metadata.
- **Categorical and boolean nulls** become an explicit "Missing" category.
- Deterministic feature engineering combines medication and condition variants (108→54 and 78→31 indicators), maps NYHA LOINC codes to ordinal 1–4, prefers first/last over redundant aggregate columns, and drops identifiers, datetimes, and near-unique identifier-like columns.

Preprocessing is provably distribution-preserving: comparing raw and preprocessed data column-by-column yields Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) statistic 0.0, total variation distance (TVD) 0.0, and missingness-rate difference 0.0 on every retained column.

The preprocessed table (4,694 × 249; 211 modelled columns after holding out 38 constants that are re-attached verbatim) was split 75/25 into training (3,520) and holdout (1,174) sets with a seeded permutation; the split manifest (row positions only) is released. **No generator, and no classifier trained during evaluation, ever saw the holdout.**

### Generative models and the run plan

Six synthesizers were trained on the training split only (Table M1), under a 31-run plan: three seeds for each non-DP model; a privacy-budget sweep ε ∈ {1, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20} for each DP model plus two additional seeds at the headline ε = 15; AIM ran on the 50 most outcome-relevant columns (selection by mean absolute association with the declared outcome variables, with the utility-evaluation targets, age, sex, and NYHA class force-included; the ranked selection is released).

| family | library | privacy | runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian copula | SDV 1.38 | none | 3 seeds |
| TVAE | SDV 1.38 | none | 3 seeds |
| CTGAN | SDV 1.38 | none | 3 seeds |
| DP-CTGAN | smartnoise-synth 1.0.8 | (ε, δ)-DP | ε sweep + 2 seeds at ε=15 |
| AIM | smartnoise-synth 1.0.8 | (ε, δ)-DP | ε sweep on top-50 columns |
| MST | smartnoise-synth 1.0.8 | (ε, δ)-DP | ε sweep + 2 seeds at ε=15 |

**DP accounting.** Column domains are treated as public metadata — they are released with the dataset in the sentinel encoding map — and passed to the DP synthesizers as explicit bounds, so no privacy budget is spent on bound discovery and the entire ε applies to synthesis at every sweep point (this also makes ε = 1 feasible at 61 continuous columns, where DP bound discovery alone would exceed the budget). δ = 10⁻⁹ (library default). AIM's measurement rounds were capped at 3× the column count (an accuracy/runtime hyperparameter that leaves the (ε, δ) guarantee unchanged); DP-CTGAN used the memory-stable configuration (300 epochs, batch 50) established for the training GPU.

Each run is independently reproducible: per-run seed, library versions, git commit, hardware, and the SHA-256 of the training file are recorded, and the SDV sampling RNG is set explicitly per run (SDV otherwise pins sampling to a fixed internal seed, which would silently void seed-replication). Each fitted generator is retained inside the secure environment (never released) so additional records, including condition-filtered cohorts, can be produced without retraining. Every synthetic dataset contains 3,520 records (matching the training size), was checked for verbatim training-row reproduction *before* sentinel decoding (zero found in all runs), and was then decoded to the released representation in which nulls are nulls.

Of 31 planned runs, 27 completed. AIM at ε ≥ 8 exceeded a 2-hour per-run compute cap even at 50 columns: AIM's measurement effort grows with the privacy budget (low-ε runs terminate early on budget exhaustion), which we report as a practical scaling result for Private-PGM-based synthesis (McKenna et al., 2019).

### Evaluation framework

All evaluations run in the released (decoded) representation unless stated otherwise, and every metric is calibrated by a real-vs-real reference:

- **Marginal fidelity.** Per-column two-sample KS (numeric, observed values) and TVD (categorical, including the Missing category), plus the mean absolute difference in numeric missingness rates; aggregated over the 211 modelled columns (the 38 verbatim constants are excluded from aggregates as copies, not modelling successes; full-schema aggregates are also reported). *Floor:* train vs holdout.
- **Association structure.** Absolute change in Spearman correlation (numeric–numeric), bias-uncorrected Cramér's V (categorical–categorical), and correlation ratio (numeric–categorical) over ~24,000 measurable pairs, plus a *fabricated-association* count: pairs with real |association| < 0.1 rendered > 0.5 synthetically. *Floor:* train-profile vs holdout-profile deltas.
- **Full-joint distinguishability.** Classifier two-sample test (C2ST; Lopez-Paz & Oquab, 2017): held-out AUC of a gradient-boosting classifier separating real from synthetic rows. *Floor:* the same test on train vs holdout.
- **Row-level clinical coherence.** 363 rules — 351 boolean implications mined from the training split (support ≥ 50; ≤ 0.2% training violations), 4 learned category-range consistencies (CKD stage vs eGFR; hyperkalemia grade vs potassium), and 8 survival-logic checks (window flags vs recorded days-to-event; follow-up bounds) — evaluated per row. *Floor:* the holdout's own violation rate.
- **Survival fidelity.** For all-cause death and heart-failure rehospitalization: a recorded days-to-event is an event; a null is administrative censoring at 1,825 days. Kaplan–Meier curves; log-rank tests vs the holdout (*floor:* train vs holdout log-rank); and effect-estimate replication — the same Cox proportional-hazards model (age, sex, NYHA, eGFR, sodium, systolic blood pressure; standardized; complete-case) fitted on real and synthetic data (Davidson-Pilon, 2019), compared by coefficient sign agreement and magnitude error.
- **Downstream utility (TSTR).** Train-synthetic-test-real (Esteban et al., 2017): classifiers trained on the real training split (baseline) and on each synthetic dataset, all tested on the holdout, for five family-diversified outcome targets including all-cause mortality; two model classes (histogram gradient boosting; regularized logistic regression) and an augmentation arm (real + synthetic vs real alone).
- **Privacy.** (i) Distance to closest training record (Gower-style mixed distance in sentinel space, so proximity requires matching values *and* missingness pattern), referenced to the holdout-to-train distance distribution — the empirical profile of an innocent unseen patient; (ii) membership inference: nearest-synthetic-record distance attack scored as AUC over training members vs holdout non-members with bootstrap 95% CIs; (iii) attribute inference from quasi-identifiers (age, sex, admission year), reported as *membership advantage* (member minus non-member attack accuracy — population-level inference is the purpose of releasing data; only member-specific advantage is leakage); (iv) singling-out and linkability risks via Anonymeter (Giomi et al., 2023).
- **Subgroup fidelity.** All marginal aggregates recomputed within sex and age strata, each against its own stratum noise floor.
- **Release gate.** An automated go/no-go check per candidate file: schema conformity, decode freshness, zero verbatim training rows, coherence within a stated policy (≤ max(10× the holdout's own violation rate, +1 percentage point)), and a distance spot-check against the holdout threshold.

The release comprises, per generative run, one CSV of 3,520 synthetic patient records with the 249-column released schema (width-limited AIM runs: their 50-column subset plus constants), named `DT4H_Synthetic_<model>[_eps<ε>]_seed<s>.csv`. Accompanying records:

- `DT4H_Codebook.md` — per-column type, description (from the UC1 feature-set metadata), observed range or category list, missingness rate, and the *meaning* of a null for that column (structural "no event" vs "not measured");

- `DT4H_Holdout_Split.json` — the seeded split manifest (row positions only);

## Technical Validation

### Marginal fidelity, calibrated by the sampling-noise floor

Two disjoint real samples of this cohort differ by KS 0.049 (numeric), TVD 0.010 (categorical), and 0.008 missingness MAD — the floor no synthesizer can honestly beat (Table 1; Figure `fig_ks_profiles`).

**Table 1.** Marginal fidelity vs the training data over 211 modelled columns (mean ± SD across seeds).

| model | KS mean | TVD mean | missingness MAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| *sampling-noise floor* | *0.049* | *0.010* | *0.008* |
| TVAE | 0.202 ± 0.003 | 0.059 ± 0.004 | 0.037 ± 0.003 |
| CTGAN | 0.310 ± 0.020 | 0.087 ± 0.016 | 0.079 ± 0.012 |
| Gaussian copula | 0.433 ± 0.008 | 0.009 ± 0.000 | 0.124 ± 0.002 |
| MST (ε=15) | 0.406 ± 0.001 | **0.0018 ± 0.0001** | **0.0028 ± 0.0002** |
| AIM (ε=5, 50 cols) | 0.315 | 0.0045 | 0.0024 |
| DP-CTGAN (ε=15) | 0.844 ± 0.017 | 0.315 ± 0.011 | 0.265 ± 0.039 |

MST's categorical fidelity and missingness reproduction at ε = 15 sit *below* the noise floor. TVAE leads numeric fidelity; its residual gap concentrates in sparsely observed laboratory values (columns observed in ≥ 90% of patients reach KS ≈ 0.05, at the floor; columns observed in ~10% are the worst), indicating a sample-size-driven rather than model-driven limit. Sub-group analysis shows uniform fidelity across sex and age strata for all models (no stratum deviates disproportionately from its own floor).

### The privacy-budget sweep

Across ε = 1 → 20 (Figure `fig_epsilon_curves`), MST improves monotonically (TVD 0.024 → 0.0014; missingness MAD 0.032 → 0.0019) and saturates above ε ≈ 10–15, empirically supporting ε = 15 as the operating point: ε = 20 buys nothing measurable. DP-CTGAN is flat and poor across the entire sweep (KS 0.73–0.90 at every ε): its failure is adversarial-training instability, not privacy noise — a budget-independent negative result for DP-GANs at this width. AIM outperforms MST on its column subset at equal ε (KS 0.326 vs 0.486 at ε = 1) but could not complete above ε = 5 within a 2-hour cap.

### Joint structure, fabricated associations, and full-joint distinguishability

The association noise floor is itself substantial at this sample size (mean |Δ| 0.065 numeric–numeric, 0.022 categorical–categorical) — a caution for the field against reporting association errors without it. TVAE preserves pairwise structure best (mean |Δ| 0.086 numeric–numeric — near the floor — with 71%/82%/89% of pairs within 0.1 across the three pair types). MST, despite near-perfect marginals, preserves only ~22–30% of pairs and **fabricates ~3,000 strong associations that do not exist in the real data** (real |association| < 0.1 rendered > 0.5) — its association error is flat across the entire ε sweep, demonstrating a model-class ceiling that privacy budget cannot buy back: MST measures only a spanning tree of pairwise marginals.

The C2ST is ≈ 1.0 for every model (floor 0.457): a classifier shown whole rows distinguishes synthetic from real essentially perfectly. Part of this reflects genuine joint imperfection; part reflects trivial tells such as value granularity (real ages and blood pressures are integer-valued; neural samplers emit continuous values), which with 211 features suffice for perfect separation. We report it as an honest bound — no current model's rows survive forensic inspection — while noting that single-row distinguishability is independent of, and in our results orthogonal to, privacy risk.

### Row-level clinical coherence

Real unseen patients violate the 363 mined rules in 0.23% of applicable checks (training split: 0.014%). Synthetic violation rates (Figure `fig_coherence`): AIM ε=5, 0.91%; TVAE, 2.0–2.3%; MST ε=15, 3.6%; Gaussian copula, 27.9–28.2%; DP-CTGAN, 52–69%. The copula result is instructive: approximately correct pairwise correlations coexist with frequent logical contradictions, because a Gaussian dependence structure cannot represent hard implications. AIM's workload-aware joint modelling yields the most internally consistent synthetic patients — a distinct capability from marginal fidelity, where MST dominates.

### Survival fidelity and effect replication

Five-year all-cause mortality is 40.2% in the training split (1,421/3,520 events) and 41.0% in the holdout (log-rank train-vs-holdout p = 0.68, the sampling-noise calibration). **All MST runs at ε ≥ 15 produce Kaplan–Meier curves statistically indistinguishable from the holdout for both endpoints** (all-cause death and heart-failure rehospitalization; log-rank p > 0.05); TVAE additionally passes for rehospitalization (Figure `fig_km_curves`). DP-CTGAN generates no deaths at all — its curve is flat at 1.0.

The Cox model on real data recovers the expected clinical epidemiology (standardized log-hazard ratios: age +0.29, NYHA +0.18, male +0.08, eGFR −0.21, sodium −0.09, systolic BP −0.06). Fitted on synthetic data, the Gaussian copula and MST (ε=15) reproduce 5/6 coefficient signs with mean absolute coefficient errors of 0.086 and 0.144 respectively; TVAE matches 5/6 signs but with an unstable magnitude on one covariate. Research conclusions at the direction-of-effect level are largely recoverable from the better synthetic datasets.

### Downstream utility

Real-data baselines on the five outcome targets range AUC 0.54–0.69 (intrinsically hard targets at this cohort size; the CV/non-CV rehospitalization-reason targets are near-complements, so effectively three independent problems). TSTR gaps vs baseline: TVAE +0.062 ± 0.021 (gradient boosting) and **+0.018 (logistic regression — near parity)**; MST ε≥15 ≈ +0.10/+0.05; the smaller gaps under the simpler model class indicate that part of the tree-model gap reflects fine joint structure rather than unusable signal. Augmenting real training data with synthetic records neither helps nor hurts (deltas −0.03 to +0.015) — synthetic data substitutes, it does not add.

### Privacy: distances and the four-attack suite

An unseen real patient's nearest-training-record distance has 5th percentile 0.063 and median 0.103 (Gower-style, in sentinel space). Across all 27 synthetic datasets: zero exact matches; the closest any synthetic record comes to any training record is 0.414 — four times the innocent-record median — and **0.0% of synthetic records fall below the holdout's 5th-percentile threshold in every run** (~5% would be expected even for a perfectly innocent generator; Figure `fig_dcr`). Nearest-neighbor distance ratios ≈ 0.99 show synthetic records sitting between real records, not locked onto individuals.

The attack suite (Table 2; Figure `fig_mia`): mean membership-inference AUC across the 27 datasets is 0.5000, with no bootstrap 95% CI excluding 0.5 (maximum single AUC 0.520, CI [0.499, 0.538]). Worst attribute-inference membership advantage: +3.6 percentage points (near noise). Singling-out risk: mean 0.006, max 0.036; linkability risk: mean 0.0006, max 0.010 (0–1 scales). Notably, attack performance is unrelated to fidelity rank — the best-fitting models are exactly as unattackable as the worst — and the DP models additionally carry their formal (ε, δ) guarantee.

**Table 2.** Privacy evidence across all 27 released datasets.

| evidence | result |
|---|---|
| verbatim training rows | 0 in every dataset |
| records closer than an unseen patient's p5 distance | 0.0% in every dataset |
| membership-inference AUC | mean 0.5000; 0/27 CIs exclude 0.5 |
| attribute-inference membership advantage | ≤ +0.036 |
| singling-out risk | mean 0.006 (max 0.036) |
| linkability risk | mean 0.0006 (max 0.010) |

### Release gating

The automated gate cleared `tvae_seed0` on all five checks (coherence 2.25% vs threshold 2.32%) and **blocked `mst_eps15_seed0` on row coherence alone** (3.63%) despite its superior marginals and survival curves — the marginal-vs-coherence dissociation enforced as governance. Both gate reports are released.

## Usage Notes

- **Which file for which purpose.** For general modelling, feature-importance work, and teaching: the TVAE datasets (gate-cleared; best joints and utility). For categorical epidemiology, missingness-pattern research, and survival-curve work under a formal privacy guarantee: MST ε = 15 — but *never* trust its cross-column correlations, and note it did not pass the row-coherence gate. AIM ε = 5 offers the most internally coherent rows over its 50-column subset. DP-CTGAN files are included for benchmark completeness only.
- **Rows are population members, not case studies.** Even the best datasets show ~10× the real data's rate of logical inconsistency per row, and whole rows are machine-distinguishable from real ones. Do not present individual synthetic records as realistic patients.
- **Nulls carry meaning** — consult the codebook before treating missingness as noise; time-to-event nulls are administrative censoring, laboratory nulls are "not ordered."
- **Effect sizes attenuate.** Expect directionally correct but shrunken associations and AUCs; confirmatory analyses require the real data under DT4H governance.
- Conditional cohorts (e.g., "500 female patients over 80") can be produced from the retained generators inside the secure environment on request; outputs pass the same release gate.

## Code Availability

The complete pipeline (13 steps: loading, profiling, preprocessing with sentinel encoding and the train/holdout split, generation under the run plan, and the eight analysis stages), the release gate, and the conditional-sampling tool are available at github.com/kkech/SyntheticDataGenerationDT4H. Every table, figure, and document in this paper regenerates via `python main.py` / `python main.py --analysis`; the exact package environment is recorded in `DT4H_Environment_Freeze.txt`, and each generation run records its seed, git commit, and training-file SHA-256. Key dependencies: SDV 1.38.0 (Patki et al., 2016), smartnoise-synth 1.0.8, scikit-learn, lifelines (Davidson-Pilon, 2019), Anonymeter (Giomi et al., 2023).

## Limitations

Single clinical site and a modest cohort (4,694 patients): absolute fidelity — particularly for laboratory values observed in few patients — is sample-size-limited, and the pipeline is designed to rerun unchanged on the forthcoming larger extract. AIM's privacy-budget sweep is incomplete above ε = 5 (a Private-PGM scaling result we report rather than hide). The C2ST result conflates genuine joint imperfection with value-granularity artifacts; a granularity-snapping decoder is future work, as is constraint-aware sampling using the released rule set, which would address the residual row-coherence gap without retraining. Cramér's V is reported without small-sample bias correction (identically for real and synthetic, so comparisons are unaffected). The membership-inference evaluation uses a distance attack; stronger learned attacks are possible in principle, though the DP models' formal guarantee bounds all of them.
